# TA rules

PA = price action. TF = time frame. VP = Volume Profile. POC = Point of Control.

The operator fills this file. Until these sections have real rules, do not turn a chart into a trade.

## How to read a chart

Read indicators from most important to least in this order: RSI, SMAs, fibs, VP (most importantly the POC, which is the large yellow node), channels, trend lines, and any other indicators mentioned after.

## Channels

Need to see how price has reacted to channel bounds and mid-line. Many tickers like to bounce from bounce to bounce or hold top channel then drop to bottom channel. Mid-channel is often a decent resistance.

## Fibonacci

There are 2 main fib setups that I use. One is a larger fib that is typically drawn from a larger price area and on a higher time frame. This is often the levels you see where only the fibs between 0 and 1 are shown. The other one focuses on shorter time frames,
but looks for extensions at the 1.618 and on. These are important to look for price to retrace to inside fibs like the .786, .618, and .5; the 0.382 is a dangerous level for a momentum flip, but never ruled out. Since fibs are mathematical levels, they can be trusted to be common levels.

## SMA colors

SMAs are to be used to find overlap with other levels, or hold the price. The best way to play them is to see if price previously respected the SMA. You can also look for mean reversion back to a daily fib if broken. Higher SMA's are typically stronger but historical PA.

Blue = 20 Green = 50. Orange = 200.

## RSI

Intraday RSI is only on the 10 minute TF. 4H and Daily RSI decide which levels are most likely in play. They do not have to align with the intraday play, and an opposing level can still work. Over 50 is bullish, over 70 is significant buyer strength. Under 50 is bearish, under 30 is significant seller strength. Those are the most import levels to look for first. Next look at RSI structure. RSI 50 is the most important zone to look at. It is a common place for bounces/rejections. A claim/break typically represents flipping of strength, but price has to follow through. RSI trends can show a reversal of trend such as making lower highs when above 50 and higher highs when below 50. One very actionable setup is when price initially falls below RSI 30 then picks back above it. After doing so, it may retest 30 and if it doesn't break it, trend is most likely flipping soon. Look for RSI to slowly climb to 50. When this happens, take note of the price to see if it made a lower low or equal low. Remember this to determine if it is more common for trend to flip depending on the low. All significant levels don't have to be perfect; there can be a little give for levels like 30, 50, and 70.

### Purple line versus yellow line versus 50

The purple line is the 14 RSI. It looks at direction faster and typically makes the move on the important levels first. The yellow line is the 14 SMA smoothing of that RSI. Look for purple above the yellow line to be bullish, below bearish.

When RSI is undecided, neither buyers nor sellers have strength. The 10 minute RSI is then the main driver of price action. These RSI rules apply to the 10 minute RSI, with heavy emphasis on how price reacts to RSI 50.

RSI is confluence. It does not block a play. A missing RSI agreement is not a reason to skip a level. Do not wait for every confluence to line up.

## Claim, hold, and lose

The levels in the operator's note are what the play is built on. Price close to a level counts. The level does not have to be tagged to the cent. A 10 minute close through the level is a claim or a lose. A wick through the level is not. A hold is a retest that does not close back through. After a break, a later candle that comes back to the level and closes on the break side is the retest entry.

Every support can flip to resistance, and every resistance can flip to support. The flip is a finished 10-minute close beyond the level by half the 14-period ATR. A close inside that band has not flipped the level. A lost support is resistance: a put on the green retest from below. A claimed resistance is support: a call on the red retest from above. A later close back through flips it again. The flipped side uses the posted numbers in that direction. The first is the next number. The far one is still the last target.

## Options

Buy the contract. The cap is $100 per contract. Look first at 0DTE or the next expiration, at the money or in the money. Those are often over the cap. A reasonable out-of-the-money contract is allowed when it can still gain into the posted target. Judge that from the theta decay and the distance from the strike to the target. Do not buy a strike so far out that the target cannot add value before theta takes it. Do not cut a contract unless the play is invalidated, or theta decay will hurt the contract significantly. Holding through a weekend, or into a higher open the next session, is theta damage for a short-dated contract.

A note can name more than one target. The first target is the next number. "Possibly" is a further target, and it comes into play if that first target is also lost or claimed. Hitting the first target does not close the trade. The contract can be trimmed if there is more than one, or rolled up the chain: a higher strike for a call, a lower strike for a put. Neither is required.

Posted levels are targets. The contract does not have to reach the target before profit is taken.

Buy calls on weakness, a red candle pushing away from the long. Buy puts on strength, a green candle pushing away from the short. Sell calls into strength. Sell puts into weakness.

## What is not a trade

No trades in between levels. Play the levels from the operator's note, including price that is close to one. RSI and a respected SMA add to the play. They do not have to line up before the trade is taken.
